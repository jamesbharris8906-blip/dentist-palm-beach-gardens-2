Maybe you want to eat corn on the cob without thinking twice. Maybe you want to smile in photos without hiding your teeth. Maybe you just want a checkup that does not leave you sore or stressed. Those goals shape the care you get. A Dentist Palm Beach Gardens who listens can spot small problems early, when they are often easier and kinder to fix.

General Dentistry

General dentistry is the base of a healthy mouth. It covers checkups, cleanings, x-rays when needed, fillings, and gum care. During a checkup, Dr. Weiss looks at each tooth, checks your gums, and talks with you about what he sees. A cleaning clears away plaque and tartar that a brush and floss can miss, and it leaves your mouth feeling fresh. If a small cavity shows up, a filling can stop it from growing into a bigger ache. If your gums bleed when you brush, the team can show you a gentler way to clean along the gum line and watch how your gums heal over time.

Most people do best with a checkup and cleaning twice a year, but your own mouth may need a different rhythm. Some people build up tartar fast. Some have gum trouble that needs closer watch. Some take medicines that dry out the mouth. Your plan should fit your mouth, your health, and your life. Bring a list of your medicines, tell the team about any new health news, and ask what you can do at home between visits. Small daily habits, like brushing twice a day and cleaning between teeth, add up to a lot over a year.

Invisalign

Invisalign uses clear trays to move teeth a little at a time. Many adults and teens like it because the trays are hard to see, they come out for meals and brushing, and there are no wires to poke the cheek. The first step is a look at your bite, your teeth, and your goals. If clear trays are a good fit for you, Dr. Weiss will map out the steps and show you what the trays will do over time. You will wear each set for about one to two weeks, then move to the next set, and you will come back for checks along the way.

Clear trays work best when you wear them as told, usually for most of the day and night. Take them out to eat and drink anything but water, brush before you put them back in, and keep them in their case when they are out of your mouth. If a tray feels tight for a day or two, that is often a sign it is doing its job. If it hurts a lot, cracks, or does not fit, call the office and ask what to do next. Straight teeth are not just about looks. They can be easier to clean, and a better bite can feel more calm when you chew.

Dental Implants

A missing tooth can change how you eat, how you speak, and how you feel about your smile. A dental implant is a small post that sits in the jaw and holds a new tooth in place. It can replace one tooth, or it can help hold a bridge or a denture. The first step is a careful look at your bone, your gums, and your health. Dr. Weiss will talk with you about whether an implant is a good choice, how many visits it may take, and how to care for it once it is in place.

Implants need time to settle and heal, so the plan is not rushed. You will get clear care steps for each stage, from the first visit to the day the new tooth is placed. Good home care matters a lot. Brush and floss around the implant just as you would a real tooth, and keep your checkups so the team can watch the gum and bone around it. With steady care, an implant can help you bite with more ease and smile without thinking about a gap.

Emergency

Tooth pain does not wait for a good time. It can show up on a workday, on a trip, or in the middle of a meal. If a tooth breaks, a filling falls out, or pain keeps you up at night, call the office at (561) 260-5575 as soon as you can. Tell the team what happened, when it started, and how bad the pain feels. They will help you know what to do next and find a time to be seen. If a tooth is knocked out, hold it by the top part, do not scrub it, and call right away for advice.

While you wait to be seen, a cold pack on the outside of the cheek can help with swelling, and warm salt water can soothe a sore spot. Do not put aspirin right on the gum, and do not try to pull a loose piece of tooth on your own. If you have swelling in the face, trouble opening your mouth, or a fever with tooth pain, get help fast. The goal of an urgent visit is simple: ease the pain, find the cause, and make a plan that keeps the tooth safe when it can be saved.

Cosmetic Dentistry

Cosmetic dentistry is about how your smile looks when you talk, laugh, and meet new people. It can cover whitening, bonding, veneers, and small shape changes that make teeth look more even. The best place to start is a talk about what bothers you. Is it color? Is it a chip? Is it a gap or a tooth that looks a bit out of line? A small change can make a big shift in how you feel, but the plan should still fit your teeth, your bite, and your budget.

Whitening can lift stains from coffee, tea, and time. Bonding can fix a small chip in one visit with a tooth-colored material. Veneers are thin shells that sit on the front of teeth to change shape or color. Dr. Weiss will show you what each choice can and cannot do, and he will talk about how long the work may last and how to care for it. The aim is a smile that looks like you, only a little brighter and more even, not a smile that looks fake or forced.

Oral Surgery

Oral surgery covers care that goes a bit deeper, like taking out a tooth that cannot be saved, removing wisdom teeth that cause pain or crowding, or getting a tooth ready for an implant. The team will walk you through each step before the day comes, so you know what to expect, how long it may take, and how to heal at home. You will hear about food, rest, and pain care in plain words, and you will have a number to call if you have a worry after you go home.

After surgery, rest is part of the care. Soft foods, cool drinks, and a calm day help the area heal. Follow the care sheet you are given, take any medicine as told, and do not smoke or use a straw unless the team says it is fine. A little swelling or soreness is common, but pain that grows worse, heavy bleeding, or a bad taste that will not go away should be checked. Call the office, describe what you feel, and let the team guide you. Good aftercare can make the next few days much smoother.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I come in for a cleaning?

Most people do well with a cleaning every six months. If your gums need closer care or tartar builds up fast, Dr. Weiss may suggest a shorter gap between visits.

Do you see people who feel scared of the dentist?

Yes. Tell the team how you feel at the start. They can slow down, talk through each step, and give you breaks so the visit feels safer.

Can clear trays fix my crowded teeth?

Many crowded smiles can be helped with clear trays, but it depends on your bite and your teeth. A checkup and a scan can show if trays are a good fit for you.

What should I do if a tooth starts to hurt at night?

Call the office in the morning at (561) 260-5575 and describe the pain. A cold pack outside the cheek and warm salt water may help while you wait.

How long does a new tooth on an implant take?

It takes time for the post to heal in the jaw, often a few months. Dr. Weiss will give you a step-by-step plan and a rough timeline at your first implant visit.

Will whitening make my teeth hurt?

Some people feel short zings of cold after whitening. The team can talk about gentler choices and tips that help your teeth feel calm again.

Yes, new patients are welcome. Call (561) 260-5575 to book a first visit, or visit the website to learn more about the office and its hours.
